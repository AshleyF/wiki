## 02 OCT 2026 Fri - AI

I watched [this Ezra interview with BillG,](https://youtu.be/A_156w0aYtU?is=vMvNv7LHxeOr0vie) spurred by his lastest piece on the dangers of AI. He's much more alarmed than Jensen Huang. He sees a turbulent period followed by a possible age of abundance, but he turbulence fill displace human jobs and we may lose control of AI or people may use it to damage the world.

I agree with this, generally. I think that human jobs will certainly disappear and I like how he separates things with "demand elasticity" when it comes to Jevons Paradox. For example, if making software becomes 100x cheaper then people may actually want 100x of it. I don't think companies like Microsoft will be able to sell 100x as much, but people will create custom bespoke software for themselves and you'll have millions of "flavors" of "Excel" for example. For an example without demand elasticity, BillG used the example of the invention of radial tires, which made tires last 10x longer but didn't cause people to drive 10x more, so jobs were lost. I think a better example is healthcare. AI will make healthcare much cheaper and better, but people won't get sick more often. Though, they may go to a doctor more often, but not 100x as often. And every industry (even software) has a saturation point where more product can't be absorbed. So, humans will simply lose jobs. Virtually every industry will be hit. First the people who deal with bits (not just software, but contracts, books, digital art, music, film, journalism, accounting, finance, insurance, education, architects, translation, ...). Then, with robots, the people who deal with atoms (every blue collar job). I think we need to start planning for how to organize society in a world without scarcity. Capitalism will not work. I'm no communist, but I simply don't see how it will work. There will always be some scarcity in the world (e.g. beachfront property, gold, ...). How those resources are distributed in a world in which humans no longer create and trade value I have no idea!

BillG's ideas around a "robot tax" and "human-reserved jobs" are silly, I think. A robot tax could make sense in the simple situation where literally a human is fired and a humanoid robot steps into their job. That's not how it will happen though. Instead, you'll have new companies forming that will just never hire humans to begin with. And the "robots" won't be as obvious as a one-to-one equivalent to a human as a biped humanoid machine. It'll just be "automation." Lots of machines of various capabilities and lots of models doing mental work without an embodyment. BillG says companies will pay FICA tax equivalent to a human. Based on what? What is the compute or physical automation equivalent to one human? His other idea of reserving some jobs for humans is a stop-gap measure. Long term, if a job can be done better/faster/cheaper with AI, then it will be done that way.

Much more dire and immediately important is AI damaging the world during this transition and beyond. I expect that it will be fine in the end, but I'm sure there will be some terrible things along the way. I don't really worry that we'll "lose control" of the AI or that recursive self-improvement will get out of control. There is a small chance that AI will so something bad while trying to accomplish what it's been asked (by humans) to do. But the much more probable thing is humans outright asking it to do evil. It's a tool that can be used for great good or great evil. It's probably the biggest lever humans have ever created. It will amplify human ambitions. Both BillG and Huang said something along the line of not shipping models until they are "safe." I don't know what that means. I understand not shipping *products* that use models when they're not safe, but models are a "building material" that can be used to make *anything.* How do you stop people from making bad things with it. It's merely a tool. I've heard it said that it's like a gun, but that's a tool made for killing (which can be good: police, etc.). AI is much more general purpose though. It's something like drones. You can take agriculture survey pictures, deliver supplies, ... or you can drop bombs or make kamakazis out of them. "Don't ship them until they're safe." How will you stop people from using them as they see fit. Some of the frontier models are hosted in the cloud by Microsoft or Google or Amazon. Those companies can put safeguards in place. I can't ask a model to help me create a biological weapon or a bomb. They have filters in place and it'll refuse. Sometimes it's the model itself that's been trained through HRLF but most of the time its a separate layer detecting such requests. The problem is that the frontier models of a few years ago are now overpowered by open source models that run *locally.* You can't enforce safeguards in that case and the power of frontier models of today will be able to be run this way in not too many years to come. If today's AI is dangerously powerful, this danger will be out in the wild in the near future. There's no stopping that. There's no regulating it.

Some of the danger comes from AI finding vulnerabilities in systems today. Taking down the grid or the banking system or whatever. I think the only defense against this is to have AI working for the good guys to find and fix the vulnerabilities before the bad guys do. Releasing the most powerful models to good people first to give them a head start is somewhat useful, but it's an ongoing battle that we'll just have to fight. It's not magic though. AI can't magically hack anything you point it at. There has to be a flaw to exploit. The same race between human hackers and security engineers has always existed. It's just gone super human now, on both sides.

The bigger danger is AI helping humans to create dangerous things. For example, it will find wonderful new medicines, and terrible new bioweapons. There's no way to create such a general purpose tool without it being able to do both. My worry is evil humans with AI. I don't worry much about "evil AI."

So, I agree that this transition period is dangerous and I still don't know what society will look like after the transition. But, I'm very hopeful that we make it through without permanently destroying ourselves and we'll come out the other end in an age of crazy abundance. People in hundred years will certainly not want a time machine to come back to now or any time earlier.

## 04 OCT 2026 - Caligraphy + Apples

You never know when an old hobbie will have a resurgance. We got an pencil for the iPad last week and today I played with ProCreate. I had learned caligraphy years ago from some YouTube channel and today I spent practically all day getting back up to speed with a copperplate miniscule & majiscule alphabet and writing all kinds of things to practice. The pencil has pressure, tilt and rotation sensors. So it's very expressive and ProCreate has all kinds of nifty controls. I like having a two-color pen that changes depending on pressure. Just as a guide so that I can see where I'm applying pressure. I learned initially on the Kindle Scribe, which automatically does thick/thin strokes based on direction. That's cheating. Controlling pressure is much more difficult, but more expressive too. For example an 'x' can't be done correctly on the Scribe.
