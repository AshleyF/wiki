# Drum Fills

A fill briefly interrupts or reshapes the groove to lead into a new phrase, section, or accent. The examples are grouped by **rhythmic shape first** and then by **orchestration**, so the same timing can be learned on the snare before being moved around the kit.

Every example uses two bars of context. The fill occupies the end of bar one; bar two lands with crash and kick on beat 1, then resumes a simple groove. The notation loops, so press **Stop** when finished.

The traditional straight-sixteenth fill is a common starting point, and landing a fill with a crash on the following downbeat is conventional practice. See [Drumeo's beginner fill lesson](https://www.drumeo.com/beat/beginner-drum-fills/) and this [collection of named fills](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/drum-fills-that-have-names/).

## Drumeo lesson grooves and fills

These transcriptions condense examples from this [Drumeo Method lesson](https://app.musora.com/drumeo/method/lesson/458192/458154) to two bars: one bar establishes the groove and the second presents the ending. Each loop begins with crash and kick so the fill resolves clearly onto the next downbeat.

### All four fills — compact four-bar loop

Each bar keeps only the active groove-and-fill bar from one example. A crash and kick begin every bar, resolving the previous fill; when the fourth fill loops, it resolves onto the first bar's crash.
